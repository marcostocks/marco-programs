Below is one complete round trip by one wallet, on a market created for this document and closed afterwards. Every address mentioned is listed in the account index at the end.

Alice buys 7 Meituan shares

She pays $1,000 from her own wallet and receives 7 position tokens back into it. This is where the money actually goes:

  1. Alice's wallet → marco_spot. She signs; her USDC leaves her wallet.$1,000.00
  2. marco_spot → MSB. The program forwards it to the licensed USDC⇄HKD ramp, keeping the $2.50 spread.$997.50
  3. MSB → broker. The MSB crosses to HKD and wires the SFC-licensed broker.off-chain
  4. Broker → custodian. The broker buys 7 shares on HKEX at $142.50 — exactly the $997.50 deployed. The custodian holds them 1:1, segregated.off-chain
  5. Custodian confirms. Only then does marco_spot mint 7 tokens into Alice's wallet.7 tokens

Her wallet, before and after:

Alice's wallet · USDC$5,000.00 → $4,000.00−$1,000.00
Alice's wallet · position tokens0 → 7receipt, frozen

Three marco_spot instructions did that, each a confirmed transaction:

  1. place_buy Alice signs. $1,000 leaves her wallet and the order opens. Verify ↗
  2. deploy_buy $997.50 is sent to the MSB — the one address the program is allowed to pay. $2.50 spread retained. Verify ↗
  3. confirm_buy Custody is attested on-chain and 7 tokens are minted to Alice. Without that evidence, nothing mints. Verify ↗

Alice sells the same position

Meituan has risen from $142.50 to $150, so the 7 shares sell for $1,050. The same path runs backwards:

  1. Alice's wallet → marco_spot. Her 7 tokens are handed back to the program — held, not yet burned, because the custodian still has the shares.7 tokens
  2. Custodian → broker. Custody releases the shares and the broker sells the 7 on HKEX.off-chain
  3. Broker → MSB. HKD proceeds are remitted and crossed back into USDC.off-chain
  4. MSB → marco_spot. The USDC returns on-chain.$1,050.00
  5. The 7 tokens are burned and marco_spot pays Alice's wallet, net of the $2.625 spread.$1,047.375

marco_vault

Pre-IPO vaults

Pooled subscriptions into IPO allocations individual investors cannot reach — initially Hong Kong listings via a licensed broker. Depositors hold a claim on the vault's net proceeds rather than the shares themselves.

One vault below, run start to finish: subscribed, deployed into a listing, sold at a gain, redeemed. Addresses are in the account index.

The lifecycle

A vault is one listing, start to finish. It advances through nine phases, each gated on a real event rather than a date — it cannot mark a listing that has not happened, and settle reads the vault's actual balance, so redemption cannot open against cash that has not arrived.

PhaseWhat happens in the deal
FundingSubscription window opens. 30 days on this deal; 90 on the vault still taking subscriptions. Capital committed, not yet spent.
SealedBook closes, at the deadline or early if the cap fills.
SourcingBroker submits the order into the syndicate book.
SourcedAllocation confirmed — and usually scaled back. Any shortfall stays in the vault and returns to holders at redemption.
DeployedCash leaves the vault for the MSB against the allocation.
LiveShares list and trade; the broker holds the position for the vault.
RealizedPosition sold; proceeds due back to the vault.
ClaimableNet proceeds returned on-chain; redemption opens.
ConcludedResidual window closes — 365 days after funding. Not yet reached: this vault is still in Claimable.

Each transition is its own transaction against the vault; the worked example below links the ones that move money. The vault's full history shows all of them.

Dave subscribes $1,000

He receives 1,000 claim tokens — the full amount, 1:1. The fee is a flat 5% of withdrawal value, charged at redemption rather than on the way in.

  1. Dave's wallet → marco_vault. He signs during the subscription window; his USDC leaves his wallet.$1,000.00
  2. marco_vault mints 1,000 claim tokens straight back into Dave's wallet, frozen. No fee is taken here.1,000 tokens
  3. The book closes and the allocation is confirmed. marco_vault → MSB, the one address the vault is allowed to pay.$1,000.00
  4. MSB → broker. The MSB crosses USDC into HKD and funds the subscription — a Hong Kong listing is paid for in HKD, not stablecoins.off-chain
  5. Broker takes up the IPO allocation and holds the shares for the vault.off-chain
  6. Shares list and are sold at +20%. The proceeds run back the same way — broker → MSB → marco_vault, crossed back into USDC — which opens redemption.$1,200.00

His wallet, after subscribing:

Dave's wallet · USDC$2,000.00 → $1,000.00−$1,000.00
Dave's wallet · claim tokens0 → 1,000receipt, frozen

Three marco_vault instructions carry the subscription, each a confirmed transaction:

  1. deposit Dave signs. $1,000 leaves his wallet and 1,000 claim tokens are minted back to him, frozen. No fee here. Verify ↗
  2. deploy_capital $1,000 is sent to the MSB — the one address the vault is allowed to pay. Verify ↗
  3. settle Reads the vault's actual USDC balance — $1,200 — and only then opens redemption. Verify ↗

Dave redeems for $1,140

He burns all 1,000 claim tokens. The flat 5% is applied here, to withdrawal value — the pool stood at $1,200, so $60 comes off and he receives $1,140.

  1. Dave's wallet → marco_vault. His 1,000 claim tokens are burned.1,000 tokens
  2. marco_vault → Dave's wallet. His pro-rata share of the $1,200 pool, less the flat 5%.$1,140.00
  3. The $60 fee stays in the vault until swept to treasury. Nothing is charged if capital never deploys.$60.00

His wallet, after redeeming:

Dave's wallet · claim tokens1,000 → 0burned
Dave's wallet · USDC$1,000.00 → $2,140.00$1,140.00

One marco_vault instruction:

  1. claim 1,000 tokens burn, $1,140 is paid to Dave and the $60 fee is retained — all in one transaction. Verify ↗
💰

Result

Dave subscribed $1,000 and redeemed $1,140 — a realised gain of $140 on a deal that returned 20%. The fee is a flat 5% of withdrawal value: the pool stood at $1,200 at redemption, so $60 was deducted. Charging it on the way out rather than the way in means subscribed capital goes to work at 1:1, and a vault that never deploys costs its holders nothing.

Off-chain orchestration

Neither program can advance past a user's instruction on its own; both require an operator. That operator is a service — not a console — with a double-entry ledger, three-way reconciliation against custodian records, and a saga runner that classifies every counterparty failure as retryable, terminal or ambiguous.

✓
Chain-driven intents. Work is created from observed on-chain deposits, never from an HTTP call — a wallet whose funds are not locked on-chain has not authorised anything. Each write reads account state first and no-ops if the transition already landed, so a lost confirmation cannot double-send.
✓
Unattended settlement. An order placed in the interface is deployed, attested and minted by the operator loop without manual intervention.
→
Remaining: the regulated counterparties. Licensed broker, custodian and FX execution behind the operator, replacing the simulated attestation with genuine confirmations, plus HSM or multisig key custody in place of a local keypair.

Solana devnet is a public test network; the USDC shown is valueless test currency and no real securities, funds, or custody are involved. This page documents a technical proof-of-concept and is not an offer, solicitation, or investment advice. Programs are unaudited and not for use with real funds.
